# Blackwater Thaw
## Castle architecture, masonry, and weathering rebuild

**Implementation scope.** Rework the castle compound and its immediate ridge in Battle Foundry. The village buildings already look good and should serve as the quality benchmark. Begin with castle meshes, prefab assembly, material instances, local terrain, and scene lighting controls. Confirm any renderer defect against a village control before changing a shared shader or default. Use the castle's existing Neuschwanstein-inspired massing and the four architectural reference photos in the earlier Blackwater Thaw package.

**Baseline.** The seven-page screenshot PDF contains 13 images, including a four-view comparison montage on page 7. Treat the montage as the most developed visible state in the supplied PDF. It already shows pale window surrounds, arched middle rows, some courtyard openings, terrace masonry, and a roof snow cap. Earlier screenshots are valuable for diagnosing the unadorned shells and ridge. Compare the current project against both before editing. This document is sized as one substantial technical chapter of the supplied graphics corpus, comparable in scope to its grunge and surface detail chapter.

**Target.** The quarry camera should see a distinct palace, principal spire, round side tower, lower red gatehouse, and wooded rock spur. At 15–50 m the viewer should see building floors, deep openings, wall thickness, slate courses, and a believable gate approach. At hand distance the stone should look laid, weathered, and locally repaired. These three views need different scales of detail. Begin with form and material separation, then add ivy, cracks, and fine wear.

Use three scales of variation. At **5–20 m**, indicate bay-wide moisture, retained versus weather-facing areas, construction changes and broad repairs. At **0.3–3 m**, show replaced stone groups, diffuse wet streaks, individual cut blocks, small pointing variation and clustered aging. At **centimeter scale**, show pores, scratches, chipped arrises and subtle roughness. World- or castle-local low-frequency noise should modify masks and tint within a restrained range. Align visible stone courses, trim and roof slate via authored UVs. Use world-aligned blending only where block direction is unimportant, such as cliff or a secondary stain layer.

Use true relief for silhouette and cast-shadow features. Use baked normals for chips and fine recessed joints. A narrow bevel and a few well-placed projecting courses give stronger shape in motion than aggressive normal strength across the full wall. Avoid fully black mortar and overly deep AO. Check the broad façade bands with albedo-only, normals, AO-only, and shadows-off views. Keep geometry-driven shadow when it clarifies the mass, and fix broad seam stripes if they come from material projection or excessively dark occlusion.

Author stone and slate as largely dielectric. Keep base color distinct from baked lighting, and roughness and normals sampled as linear data when integrated in the project's material pipeline. If using packed maps, document channel assignments and retain mip-safe edges for dark mortar and thin trim. Test initial dry stone roughness around **0.65–0.85** and localized damp stone around **0.40–0.65** according to renderer response. Water in cracks and shiny cut metal should have narrower local highlights. Inspect the roof and wall at fixed noon overcast and from a darker normal play route before tuning balances.

## 5. Ivy, damp, cracks and repairs

### Map causes before adding decals

Create an elevation paintover or mask showing north or shade-facing surfaces, joints where snow sits, roof runoff, sills, rising damp, overhanging features, displaced masonry, and roots. Put moisture under overflow paths and at the shaded bottom of masonry. Put repairs at access points, altered voids and repeated exposed corners. Keep the cleanest stone where snow slides or the wind scours. Make aging distinct on red masonry, ashlar, slate and rock.

| Layer | Placement and shape | Near and far representation |
|---|---|---|
| Damp base and runoff | Irregular darkened area above soil and paving. Start narrow vertical tails below sills, gutters, flashing and cornice joints. Vary tail length and feather it with the wall microrelief. | Asset masks can drive mild darkening and roughness changes together. Keep large stains readable against surrounding stone at 30–70 m. |
| Ivy stems and leaves | Begin at real root beds, retaining-wall seams, or a planter or abutment. Send one primary rope up a sheltered return and lateral stems along ledges. In late winter use bare stems and sparse dark olive leaves, with a few dense protected pockets. | Curves or slim meshes for nearby stems, alpha-tested leaf clusters for near and middle distance, and broad baked coverage for distant walls. |
| Moss, algae and lichen | Put green-gray moss on shaded foundation tops and wet ledges. Put smaller muted lichen colonies across exposed old stone. Keep the palace walls generally pale in the vista. | Use local masks and small material offsets. Small moss shelf geometry where thickness breaks a retaining-wall outline. Fade fine flecks with stable mips. |
| Cracks and chips | Start at corners of older openings, wall junctions, stepped foundations and isolated weathered caps. Follow mortar in short stepped paths and split at occasional block edges. Use one or two readable repairs or larger cracks on hero surfaces. | Near cracks get darkening, shallow bevel or normal relief, and narrow roughness changes. Give only selected severely damaged trim true chipped silhouettes. |
| Repairs and repointing | Add one former opening filled with distinct masonry, a few replaced blocks, slightly brighter repointing and a changed sill stone. Use a coherent extent and maintain stable structure. | Preserve the repair region and geometry boundaries in medium LODs. Fine tooling can disappear with distance. |
| Mineral, rust and soot marks | Use salt bloom near an evaporating damp band, thin rust runs below metal anchors, and small soot halos near actual vents. | Low-opacity decals or material masks with physically aligned causes. Keep each mark quieter than the architectural relief. |

### Quantity and hierarchy for the first art pass

- Put **two or three** ivy systems around the compound first, with one strong cluster on a sheltered side of the lower gatehouse or retaining terrace, a smaller one at the courtyard return, and one sparse patch or bare stem near a tower base. Start one hero climb around **3–6 m tall**. Most palace ashlar remains exposed. Anchor every lowest visible stem and keep the central gate trim, principal tower belt and key windows readable.
- Begin with **one** prominent old repair event on the accessible courtyard or gate area, then add scattered stone replacements. Use subtle lime mortar and color shifts where the work has aged. Keep cracks grouped around the repair, a buttress join, or a grade shift. Test small interior cracks around **3–12 mm** near inspection range, and let distant visibility come from the discolored surrounding material. Use darker texture detail beneath a cap only where it reveals real depth.
- For ivy, author a few asymmetric stem sets with openings through the foliage. Let branches favor real cracks or supports but occasionally cross stone faces. Rotate and scale leaves per cluster, preserve opaque shapes in alpha mips, attach frost or snow to a small number of real upward-facing leaf groups, and constrain wind motion where the branches contact the wall.
- First check a clean façade with no weathering decals in overcast light. Add each weathering family individually. Stop increasing coverage when it starts hiding the cut stone or makes a dry upper facade look marshy. Compare dry and wet versions on a simple material turntable or controlled camera shot.

A reusable authoring approach can combine manually painted cause masks and broad spatial breakup. Keep each mask individually inspectable. For a proposed implementation, start from:

```text
sheltered_growth = root_reach * moisture * (0.45 + 0.55 * shelter)
runoff_intensity = painted_flow * (0.65 + 0.35 * low_frequency_variation)
wetness = saturate(base_damp + runoff_intensity + local_melt)
```

Use growth to control stem and leaf placement and very localized color. Use wetness to modulate the material. Paint runoff downward from a collector or exit point, with splits at edges and a soft termination. Store parameters with the castle asset or material instance so the simplified meshes maintain the large color pattern.

## 6. Roofs, cold weather and the grounds

### Slate and snow behavior

Rebuild one dormer and adjacent roof bay before repeating the kit. Use a real slate edge, shadow gap, painted or timber fascia, dark underside, flashing at the cheek, and a cap or small gutter where appropriate. Add ridge tiles, minor snow overhang and change in normal where the snow meets the slate. Make towers have a recognizable crown: a shaped projecting belt and brackets or corbels where visible, a short upper ring with vents or arches, and a roof edge with depth. Differentiate principal spire, lateral tower and gatehouse roofs by height ratio, pitch, and detail at the band.

Paint the roof accumulation at broad scale with topology, wind exposure and snow retention. Upward-facing horizontal terrace caps hold more snow than exposed steep slate. Roof valleys, shelter behind dormers, gutters and stepped gable caps hold localized deposits. Some steep outer tower faces should remain exposed. Start with **2–8 cm nominal** visible caps on sheltered flat elements where closeups can see the lip. Keep steep roof patches thinner and broken. Add slush or icicles near melt and drip points if they remain legible. Compare with the page 7 montage and remove any visually uninterrupted white strip that ignores dormer cheek geometry or roof sheds. Use displacement or separate mesh only where it changes shadow and silhouette.

### Cliff and terrace construction

1. Treat the spur as a spine of **three to five large rock masses** with an upper terrace, side clefts, and shelves. Use different angle and silhouette per section. Shape fracture planes, scree fans, and stepped bench transitions rather than convergent vertical streaks on one steep smooth mesh. Sample the rock in triplanar or cliff-aligned coordinates where stretched cliff UVs are visible. Keep contact between exposed bedrock and foundations continuously visible.
2. Carry the platform wall into each side of the mountain. Use a series of buttresses accenting changing slope pressure and height, visibly wider near their base, plus cap blocks, repaired courses and subtle weep outlets into real lower terrain. Retain sufficient wall thickness and safe navigation clearance in any accessible area. Give the curved wall segmented stone courses and cap geometry following the bend, with steps in height where the road grades.
3. Remove the visible void or band from the narrow-end view. Classify it with world wireframe, terrain tile IDs and a fog-off orbit. If it is an exposed terrain skirt or clip seam, extend or stitch the relevant geometry and cover scenic edge with coherent geology. If it is a proxy or projection artifact, repair the responsible asset or boundary draw. Recheck from all documented viewing positions.
4. Plant firs in families of heights on shelves and on ground that can hold soil. Use small firs and bare shrubs in damaged cut banks and protect open rock faces. Anchor every near trunk to the grade. Leave a stone and snow gap immediately below part of the palace to clarify the ground connection. Place taller silhouettes lower on the slopes so major towers retain air behind them.
5. Dress the forecourt with irregular paving, broad wheel or foot clearance through packed winter ground, scattered snow against the parapet and thaw along the gate threshold. Add only a few motivated props such as a gate lantern, a weathered door beam, stone drain covers and repair timber. In a traversable courtyard, use paths and local props to show where people previously entered and serviced the buildings.

## 7. Light, composition and material readability

If base color is too dark, shift the palace toward pale neutral limestone and separate trim by finish. If neutral base color is already correct, inspect AO buildup, terrain shadowing, probe placement and whether the distant object receives the expected diffuse sky contribution.

Keep the gatehouse red enough to identify by color in gray light, with pale window surrounds and a stone portal. Both main facades should preserve shape in an SDR grayscale capture. Adjust the individual castle asset first when the village test looks healthy. Calibrating heavy ambient occlusion or exposure globally can erase the detail in every small building.

## 8. Implementation, detail budgets, and verification

### How to build it in the project

1. Reuse the castle base mesh and existing editor groupings where practical. Create window families, wall panels with apertures, corner returns, belt courses, tower bases and crown pieces, dormer pods, gate archivolts, terrace caps and snow meshes from the staged elevations. Export representative parts through the existing GLB pipeline and confirm scale, pivot, normals, tangents, hard edges and seam normals before placing the full set.
2. Set wall panels to a common scale and building-local elevation. Author corner stone as returns wrapping the corner. Share asset definitions for frames, sills, slim mullions, cover trims and opaque backing. Keep snow or ivy actors tied to the associated asset transform as needed so edits preserve placement. The intended update should survive save, reload and reimport.
3. Assign material IDs for pale body stone, pale cut trim, darker base stone, gatehouse brick, slate, build-up snow, wood and glass. Reuse project atlas or trim-sheet patterns and authored masks.
