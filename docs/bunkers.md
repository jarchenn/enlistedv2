# Bunkers

Six bunkers live in the **Bunkers** palette category, plus one crew station in **Field guns**. They are all generated by `tools/catalog_bunkers.mjs`:

| Asset id | Name | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bunker_mg_casemate` | Machine-Gun Casemate | 8 × 5.8 m, roof 3.8 m | Continuous slit across the front, wrapping both corners |
| `bunker_command_shelter` | Command Shelter | 9.4 × 7.4 m, roof 3.8 m | Two rooms and a gas lock; front slit and a rear loophole |
| `bunker_large_casemate` | Large Bunker (Casemate) | about 18 × 12 m, 6.0 m tall | Atlantic Wall gun casemate |
| `bunker_observation_post` | Observation Post | 4.5 × 4.5 m | Earth-banked; armoured flap over the slit |
| `bunker_field_shelter` | Field Shelter | 5 × 4 m | Log Unterstand under an earth cover |
| `bunker_pak_casemate` | PaK Casemate (R612 type) | 8.4 × 7.7 m, roof 3.9 m | Gun casemate for the PaK 40 |
| `gun_mg42_embrasure` | MG 42 on embrasure mount | crew station | Placed with the MG casemate |

## Companions

An asset can list `"companions": [{ "asset", "pos", "yaw" }]`, given in asset space. When the host is placed from the palette, `App::placeCompanions` places each companion as an ordinary instance on the host's floor. After that they are independent instances: they save and load like anything else, and they don't move with the host.

| Host | Companions |
|---|---|
| MG casemate | the MG 42 embrasure mount, behind the front slit |
| PaK casemate | the PaK 40 at the embrasure, and a PaK 40 HE crate |

Maps and tests that call `placeInstance` directly get only the host.

## Crew stations in bunkers

- **Crew position.** A gunner or handler stands on the floor under their crew position: a bunker floor if there is one, otherwise the terrain (`crewFloor` in `src/station.cpp`).
- **MG 42 embrasure mount** (`kind: "mg42"`).
  - A belt-fed MG station like the M2HB: 1200 rpm at 755 m/s, 50-round belts with 250 in reserve, 4.6 s reload, AUTO/SEMI.
  - The pivot is 0.2 m behind the slit and the gunner stands at the grips. Traverse is ±55°, elevation −5/+7°; at the edges of the traverse the band of the stepped reveal limits elevation.
  - The bore is 1.584 m and the sight line 1.62 m above the floor, inside the shared slit band.
  - The gun is drawn from the user-supplied MG 42 GLB in the mount's cradle (`src/app.cpp`). The mount itself is a steel pedestal, swivel head, fork and cradle; no procedural gun geometry.
  - It resupplies from crates holding `792_belt50`.
- **PaK casemate.**
  - The PaK 40's trunnion sits 0.93 m behind the inner face of the 1.5 m front.
  - The gun embrasure's throat is 2.0 × 1.08 m. It widens outward in three 0.5 m bands, by 0.33 m per side and 0.24 m upward per band.
  - Each band is sized for the bore at its far end with traverse and elevation combined, so the bore clears it over the gun's full traverse (±32.5°) and elevation (−5/+22°).
  - The gun stands deployed on the ground-level floor, behind double steel gun doors.
  - The commander's slit uses the shared slit height.

## Camouflage netting

The MG casemate, command shelter, large casemate and PaK casemate carry camouflage netting (`camoNet()`):
- slightly tilted sheets over the roof;
- skirts hung from the roof edges that flare out toward the ground, on the rear and flanks only and never in front of a slit;
- a short festoon along the firing front's roof fascia.

Netting is **visual only**: no collision, and rounds and view rays pass through.

The material is `camo_net`, shader pattern 16 `net`: disruptive olive, brown and dark blotches with leafy scrim lumps and garnish tufts. It is part-local, so the pattern follows each sheet.

## Regenerating

Run `node tools/build_bunkers.mjs`. It rewrites only `content/assets/bunker_*.json`. **Don't use `tools/build_assets.mjs` for bunker work**: it rewrites every catalogue asset and `content/weapons/*.json`, which wipes hand edits.

## Construction

Everything is procedural primitives in asset space. The front faces +Z and the ground is at y = 0.
- **Shell.** `shell()` builds four walls with `wall()`. Every wall bay becomes a destructible concrete panel (see [destruction.md](destruction.md)).
  - The walls are 1.2 m thick on the MG casemate and the command shelter.
  - The front and back walls own the corners.
  - There is a 2 cm `concrete_wash` skin inside.
- **Rounded box (`t: "r"`).** A box whose every edge is rounded by `rad` (see [asset-format.md](asset-format.md)).
  - Used for the footing step, the roof slabs, the corner piers, the pilasters, the entrance wing piers and hoods, and the large casemate's plinth, nose, hood, cheeks and dome.
  - Its collision is a Jolt box with the same convex radius, so what you see is what you bump into.
  - **Rounded parts are static.** They never become damage bays.
- **Heavy roof slab.** A rounded slab 1.0–1.2 m thick that overhangs the firing sides by 0.6–0.8 m and the rear by 0.25–0.45 m. It casts the shadow line over the slits.
- **Footing.** A rounded step 0.28 m wider than the walls, 0.22 m above the ground (0.35 m on the large casemate).
- **Secondary massing.**
  - Rounded corner piers stand 0.15 m proud of the wall faces. On the MG casemate's front corners they only rise to the slit.
  - Pilasters break the long walls.
  - The rear door is recessed between two wing piers under a hood, with a concrete step and a free-standing blast wall in front.
- **No earth wedges.** The MG casemate, command shelter and large casemate stand free on their footing. The large casemate only has low, tapered sand drifts (`drift()`) against its walls, at most 0.85 m high and a 1:3.5 slope, and walkable. The observation post and field shelter keep their earth cover.

## Slit height

`SLIT` in `tools/catalog_bunkers.mjs` is the single shared constant for every viewing slit and loophole: `{ sill: 1.47, top: 1.77, w: 0.5, eye: 1.62 }`, in metres above the bunker's interior floor.

- **Measured reference.** The old command-shelter loophole was 0.50 × 0.30 m, from 1.25 to 1.55 m above its floor.
- **Why it moved.** The standing eye is 1.62 m (`content/settings/movement.json`), which sat 7 cm *above* the top of that loophole. Through a wall over 1 m thick, a standing player could only look down at nearby ground and never saw the horizon.
- **The rule now.** The measured 0.30 m opening height is kept and centred on the standing eye: sill 1.47 m, top 1.77 m. Narrow slits (the command shelter's front slit and loophole, the field-shelter aperture) use the measured 0.50 m width; the long slits are as wide as their wall needs.
- **Where it applies.**
  - MG casemate (front and both sides)
  - command shelter (front slit and loophole)
  - large casemate embrasure
  - observation post slit (1.5 m wide)
  - field-shelter aperture

A crouched eye (1.05 m) is below the sill, so you see out standing.

## Stepped reveals

`slit()` builds a Treppenscharte: the opening is narrowest at the inner face (the throat, exactly `SLIT`) and widens outward in steps.
- **Step size:** 6.5 cm top and bottom and 12 cm at each closed end per depth band.
- **Bands:** three bands across a 1.2 m wall, two across the large casemate's 0.6 m embrasure wall.

With the eye about 0.5 m behind the inner face, the reveal never blocks a view ray inside about ±8° vertically and ±14° (narrow slits) to ±20° (long slits) horizontally. `--selftest-update` checks this with ray fans.

The step liners are thin static concrete boxes. The wall itself is cut with the outermost opening, so its bays stay destructible.

## The individual bunkers

### MG casemate
- **Slit.**
  - One continuous slit runs the full 8 m front.
  - It turns both front corners and continues over the front half of each short side (from z = 0 to the corner).
  - The rear half of each side and the rear wall have no slit.
- **Corner posts.** A 0.4 × 0.4 m post sits in each corner of the slit, set back 0.4 m, so the lintel and roof visibly bear on it.
- **Entrance.** A recessed steel rear door behind a blast wall.
- **Interior.** MG table under the front slit, ammunition, bench, shelf, two lamps.

### Command shelter
- **Layout.** Same layout as before: gas lock with a gas-tight door, and a map room with radio desk, bunks and cabinet.
- **Shell.** Rebuilt as a free-standing 1.2 m shell with roof slab, corner piers, front pilasters and a recessed entrance.
- **Openings.**
  - A stepped front observation slit.
  - The steel-flapped rear loophole covering the entrance passage. It is now stepped and at the shared height.

### Large casemate
Modelled on an Atlantic Wall gun casemate (reference photo supplied by the user).
- **Main block (rear, tall).**
  - x −2.5…9.0, z −6.0…−0.3, 1.2 m walls up to 4.9 m.
  - Heavy rounded roof slab from 4.9 to 6.0 m, overhanging 0.8 m at the front and flanks.
  - Holds the crew room (bunks, table, shelf).
- **Rear entrance.** A steel door at x = 3 between wing piers and a hood, two steps, and a blast wall 3 m behind.
- **Forward casemate (low).**
  - x −8…2, projecting to z = 4.6.
  - Rounded plinth, a pillowed lower nose and rounded cheeks.
  - A rounded hood from 2.95 to 3.85 m that overhangs 1.1 m past the nose.
- **Embrasure.**
  - A 4.4 m throat at the shared slit height, in a 0.6 m embrasure wall set 1.1 m back.
  - The shadowed recess under the hood runs between the cheeks.
  - Behind it is the firing room (6.4 × 3.2 m, 2.45 m ceiling) with ammunition, shell rack and bench.
  - A steel door connects it to the crew room.
- **Surroundings.** A half-buried rounded dome on the east flank, and sand drifts against the west flank, the rear, the east flank and the nose.

## Material

The pattern is `bunker` (shader pattern 15, `src/render.cpp`), used by `concrete` and `concrete_wash` in these bunkers. `concrete_floor` and other assets keep pattern 11.

- **Formwork:**
  - 0.24 m horizontal board formwork, with board-to-board tone in staggered 3 m lengths;
  - joint lines and wood-grain imprint;
  - pour-lift lines every 1.2 m;
  - form-tie holes on a 0.9 × 0.72 m grid;
  - large-scale mottling.
- **Weathering:**
  - darker, browner staining at the foot of every vertical face;
  - a darker band and run-off streaks below the roof lip;
  - lichen patches on top faces.
- **Roof-lip height.** The material's `grain` field carries the lip height in decimetres, set per asset by `bunkerMaterials(A, lip)`. The large casemate's `concrete_hood` uses the hood height.

The pattern is all procedural in the shader: no textures and no extra draw calls.

## Destruction

- **Damage bays.** Box walls are damage-field bays (reinforced concrete, resistance 1.2 per metre, about four HE hits per spot): the shells, the slit sill and lintel bands, the MG casemate's corner posts, the large casemate's main-block, flank, rear and embrasure walls, and the blast walls.
- **Static parts.** Rounded parts (roof slabs, footing, piers, pilasters, hood, nose, plinth, dome) and the thin reveal liners never take local damage. When the wall behind them is breached they stay where they are.
- **Breaches.**
  - A breach in a 1.2 m wall, widened to standing height, can be walked through. `--selftest-damage` does this on a rotated MG casemate.
  - Crouching through a breach works once it is open about 1.25 m high (crouched capsule 1.2 m plus padding). `--selftest-damage` crawls through a 1.26 m breach in 1.2 m of concrete.

## Tests

- `--selftest` (suite A) walks every bunker's walkthrough at a rotation: doors, flaps, slits, interiors, and the floor height at each step.
- `--selftest-update`, crew stations:
  - the companions are placed;
  - the MG 42 mount's and the casemated PaK 40's bores clear their openings at every traverse/elevation limit;
  - each gunner stands on the bunker floor (the MG 42 sight in the slit band);
  - a real burst or shell leaves the bunker;
  - camouflage nets have no collision.
- `--selftest-update`, for each of seven slits (MG front, both MG sides, command front, command loophole, large embrasure, R612 commander's slit):
  - from the standing eye at 1.62 m above the floor, a 5 × 3 fan of eye rays clears 10 m;
  - an ADS round leaves the bunker, for the MG front, command front and large embrasure.
  - The update suite also checks that no turf berms remain on the three free-standing bunkers.
- `--selftest-damage`:
  - the rotated MG casemate's rear bay survives one HE hit, then breaches, then is walked into;
  - a second copy's rear wall is opened only to crouching height and crawled through crouched;
  - the large casemate's rear wall survives one HE hit, then breaches;
  - an AT round damages its embrasure wall;
  - its rounded parts are counted.
- **Screenshots:** `NMK_CAPTURE_SET=bunkers NormandyMapmaker.exe --capture-materials <tag>` writes `selftest_out/mat_<tag>_<bunker>_<view>.png` from fixed asset-space cameras. The large casemate's `reference` view uses the reference photo's angle.
